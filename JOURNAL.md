# 4/22/2026 3 AM - finished secondary schematic and began working on the PCB

_Time spent: 5.25h_

I decided to combine a motor driver, the `TB6612FNG`, with my breadboard. This might sound pretty dumb because anyone could just connect the two components on a breadboard, but I don't have the space for that on my limited chasis. 

Before editing the schematic, I had to research into the specifics of the `TB6612FNG` as well as find out how to power the board with a 12V supply. I ended up having to go down a rabbit hole of buck converters but found one simple enough: the `AP63203WU-7`. This needed a 4.7uH inductor to smoothen it out to a steady 3.3V, in addition to more decoupling capacitors.

I decided to create a secondary hierarchical sheet and implement the new power systems and motor driver there. I connected a +12V net label to a barrel jack sized for the 2.1mm x 5.5mm plug on my battery pack, and ran that same rail into the buck converter input. The buck output feeds into a diode OR-ing circuit alongside the existing `MCP1700x-330xxTT` from the USB-C section, so the board powers itself from whichever source is available without any manual switching.

![image](https://stasis.hackclub-assets.com/images/1776827098548-26gt9n.png)

The `TB6612FNG` sits on the same sheet, with its VM motor supply rail drawing directly from the 12V barrel jack and its logic VCC pulling from the 3.3V rail. All 7 control signals --- AIN1, AIN2, BIN1, BIN2, PWMA, PWMB, and STBY --- are wired to GPIO2 through GPIO8 on the RP2040 via global labels crossing between schematic sheets. Motor outputs connect to two screw terminals for easy wiring to whatever motors end up on the chassis.
